Raw shell or finished suite

Across the twelve buildings we represent, no two floor plates deliver the same way. Some tenants want the shell as we found it — brick, timber, and steel left to do the work. Others want a suite they can move into on day one. We track which floors qualify for historic tax credits and how that history constrains the build-out, so what we quote is what the building will actually allow.

Delivered Raw

  • Brick left exposed, patched only where structurally necessary
  • Original timber posts and beams, sanded but not refinished
  • Structural steel and mechanical runs left visible overhead
  • Window walls restored to original sash proportions and glazing
  • Base electrical and life-safety systems brought to code, nothing further
  • Original wood plank or poured concrete floors, unsealed

Delivered Finished

  • Partitions, ceilings, and lighting built to the tenant's plan
  • HVAC distributed across the full floor plate, zoned by suite
  • Kitchenette and restrooms complete and code-compliant
  • Engineered flooring or carpet laid over the original subfloor
  • Paint, millwork, and door hardware complete throughout
  • Data and electrical drops placed to the tenant's layout
Why a mill building

What creative and studio tenants are actually looking for

We represent roughly twelve buildings at a time, which is deliberately narrow. It means we can tell you the elevator capacity, the column spacing, and the tax-credit status of every floor we show — not read it off a listing sheet.

Freight elevators

Every building we represent still runs its original freight car, or a rebuilt one sized to match. That matters when a studio is moving a CNC router, a server rack, or a run of sample furniture in for a client review — not just a desk and a chair.

Window walls

Mill construction put glass on two or three exposures to light the looms without electricity. The same walls now put daylight twenty-five feet into a floor plate, which is the difference between a studio that needs task lighting at 2pm and one that doesn't.

Floor plates built for one tenant or six

Column spacing between eighteen and twenty-two feet leaves open runs that work undivided for a single design firm, or subdivide cleanly into separate suites without an awkward leftover bay. We know which of our twelve buildings can do which, and how far a partition wall can move before it hits a column line.
