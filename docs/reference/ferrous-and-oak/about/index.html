Our Story

Ferrous & Oak started from a plain observation: mill buildings are not generic commercial stock, and treating them that way does a disservice to owners, residents, and tenants alike. A converted textile mill has a structural logic all its own — the spacing of the timber posts, the capacity of the freight elevator, the way the window walls throw light across a floor plate at four in the afternoon. Most brokerages list this kind of property alongside everything else. We decided not to.

We represent roughly twelve buildings at a time, across Providence's mill corridors, and we know each one the way an owner knows a house they built themselves. We can tell you which floors carry which loads, which units get afternoon light and which stay cool through August, and which buildings still have unused historic tax credits attached to them. That kind of knowledge does not come from a listing sheet. It comes from walking the sub-basements, riding the freight elevators, and sitting through the closings.

Narrowing the focus this far means turning away business that does not fit — office parks, new construction, anything without brick and timber underneath it. In exchange, when a client asks a specific question about a specific building, we have an answer, because one of us has already been there.

What We Know

We represent roughly twelve buildings at a time, on purpose. That number is small enough that every principal at this brokerage has walked every floor, ridden every elevator, and read every historic-credit filing attached to the properties we list — in Providence's mill districts, that depth of knowledge is the product.

Floor Plates

Column spacing, load capacity, ceiling height by bay — we know which floors in which buildings suit a trading desk, a photography studio, or a two-bedroom conversion, and which don't.

Freight Elevators

Original freight cars still run in about half our buildings. We know their weight limits, their cycle times, and which ones need a service contract before a tenant moves equipment through them.

Window Walls

Six-over-six mill sash, replacement steel, or a later infill — window condition drives heating cost and light quality more than any other single factor. We've stood in front of every run.

Tax-Credit History

Federal and state historic credit filings, easements, and compliance periods attached to each building. This paperwork determines what an owner can alter and what a tenant can expect to stay unchanged.

The Two of Us

Marguerite Doyle

Principal Broker, Residential

Marguerite has walked every loft in the current portfolio, most of them more than once. She can tell you which units hold heat through a Providence February, which freight elevators still run on the original relay switches, and which window walls face the river light in the afternoon. Before Ferrous & Oak she spent nine years appraising historic tax-credit properties for a preservation trust, work that taught her to read a building's renovation history in its bones.

Theo Kessler

Principal Broker, Commercial

Theo handles the office side, matching creative firms to floor plates that fit how they actually work. He came from commercial leasing in Boston's Fort Point district, another mill district, before deciding Providence's buildings deserved a broker who understood tax-credit timelines as well as square footage. He knows which floors take a full build-out and which are better left raw.
