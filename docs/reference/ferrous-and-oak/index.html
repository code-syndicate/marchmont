Why mill buildings

We represent roughly twelve of these buildings at a time — enough to know each one's floor plates, freight elevators, window walls, and tax-credit history without consulting a file. That familiarity is the reason the same building type works for both a resident and an office tenant.

Fourteen-foot ceilings

Loom rooms and drive-shaft runs demanded height. What's left is a ceiling line few new buildings can justify structurally or financially — factory sash windows stacked to the cornice, timber or riveted-steel framing left exposed overhead.

Historic tax credits

Federal and Rhode Island historic rehabilitation credits factor into nearly every acquisition we structure. We track which buildings still carry unused credit basis and which have already been fully monetized by a prior owner.

Floor plates built for both

The wide, column-spaced bays engineered to carry textile machinery convert without alteration to an open residential loft or a collaborative office floor — the same plate, two tenancies.
