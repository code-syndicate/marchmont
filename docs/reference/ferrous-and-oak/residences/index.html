What Living Here Is Actually Like

We have walked every building we represent, including through a Providence winter. What follows is what we tell prospective residents before they tour, not after they sign.

Heating

Most of the twelve buildings we represent run on converted steam or radiant systems original to their mill era, retrofitted rather than replaced. Radiators run hot and are slow to respond — expect to crack a factory window in January more often than you would expect. A few buildings have newer mini-split systems layered in during their tax-credit renovation; ask which one you are looking at before you sign.

Light

This is the real argument for mill living. Window walls fourteen feet tall on a good exposure mean no lamps are needed until late afternoon most of the year. That said, north-facing units on lower floors go flat and cool by December, and units backed against an adjacent mill building lose an hour or two of direct sun compared to a corner unit.

Noise

Open floor plates and exposed structure carry sound. A freight elevator two floors down is audible as a low mechanical hum, not a slam. Shared floors with office tenants during the day mean footsteps and rolling chairs overhead if you are in a mixed-use building. Street-facing units near a mill's original loading dock pick up delivery traffic in the morning.

Parking

Almost none of these buildings were built with parking in mind, because they were not built for cars at all. Most residences come with one off-site permitted spot in a nearby lot, sometimes a short walk away, and a handful of buildings have no dedicated parking whatsoever. If a second car matters, say so before we schedule a walkthrough.
